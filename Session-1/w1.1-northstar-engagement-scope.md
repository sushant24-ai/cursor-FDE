## One clear opinion

**Scaffolding first.** They already bought and adopted Cursor. The missing piece is shared practice. If I sell factory or company-wide Bugbot now, I skip the floor they need.

## Customer snapshot

Northstar Logistics is B2B SaaS logistics, Series C, roughly $80M ARR. About 250 engineers across 30 teams. Stack is a TypeScript monolith plus Go services. CI/CD is modern and deploys are fast. They want to move quickly. No security or compliance people are in the Phase 1 room.

Cursor has been live about three months. Roughly 85% of engineers are active. Past autocomplete. Local agents are in wide use. There is still no org standard: no central rules, no sub-agents, each team making it up.

**Problem in one line:** Gains are real but uneven, so "level everyone up" is a Phase 1 scaffolding problem.

---

## Engagement (crisp)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Primary pillar** | Scaffolding (Rules, Skills, Sub-agents, Hooks) |
| **Duration** | 3 weeks embed + 1 week light handoff |
| **Champion** | VP of Engineering |
| **Daily co-builders** | 2 platform staff engineers |
| **Pilot** | 3-5 teams (mix flying + lagging); pull 2-3 flying leads in week 2 as pattern donors |
| **Day-30 metric** | Lagging cohort within ~20% of flying on agreed confidence/usage-quality; 100% of pilot on Team Rules + documented skills/sub-agents; platform can add a rule without me |
| **Phase 2 (week 5+)** | Bugbot / Cursor code review on the TS monolith + 1-2 high-churn Go services, after review standards live in rules / `BUGBOT.md` |

**Why not the other pillars as primary**

- Not enterprise readiness: rollout is done (~85% active).
- Not async / cloud agents: org is not sync-proficient yet; fix uneven practice first.
- Not factory / SDK: too early without a shared baseline.
- Not Trust & Governance as the lead: security is not in the room. Keep Phase 1 to engineering standards. Governance stays cross-cutting and light.

---

## Weekly outcomes

**Week 1 - Diagnose and baseline**  
Shadow one flying team and one lagging team. Inventory whatever prompts or rules exist. Draft Team Rules (enforceable) plus a monorepo vs Go service split. Agree the day-30 metric with VP Eng.

**Week 2 - Ship primitives**  
Land Team Rules and project rules for the TS monolith and Go services. Add at least two skills (PR review playbook, service-onboarding map) and two sub-agents (`explore-monolith`, `go-service-review`). One `beforeShellExecution` or `afterFileEdit` hook as a guardrail pattern, not a compliance program. Pilot on the 3-5 teams.

**Week 3 - Level-up and handoff**  
Office hours plus a short playbook for the rest of the org. Platform staff own the primitives in repo and Cursor team dashboard. Measure pilot vs baseline. Soft handoff; I stay on-call for questions only.

**Week 4 (light)**  
Platform runs the wider rollout. I review metrics and scope Phase 2 Bugbot only if Week 3 landed.

---

## What we leave behind

- **Rules:** Enforced Team Rules (TS + Go conventions, PR expectations, no secrets in chat). Repo rules for monolith and Go services.
- **Hooks:** One project hook (ask on risky shell, or format-after-edit) as the pattern. Platform extends it.
- **Skills / sub-agents:** At least two of each in a platform "Cursor standards" area.
- **Where it lives:** Team Rules and marketplace skills in the Cursor dashboard; `.cursor/` in the repos. Nothing laptop-only.

---

## Risks I will name in the room

- **Change management:** Flying teams may resist "standardization." Harvest their patterns into Team Rules. Do not invent from scratch. Lagging teams get office hours, not a PDF.
- **Multi-stack:** Monolith + many Go services. One vertical slice of primitives both stacks can use. No rewrite.
- **Compliance:** Security is not here. No Autofix-at-scale, no broad MCP trust, no "we're compliant" claims. Flag Privacy Mode / MCP allowlist as a Phase 1.5 talk with security.
- **Out of scope:** Software factory SOW, company-wide Bugbot on day one, custom SDK, pretending 30 teams transform in three weeks.
